# Persona Impact Notes: how the cast and the Keeper change Intro to Ch3

Started 26 Oct 2026. Thinking notes, not decisions. Add-only. His answers go under each item with a date.

Read against: Introduction and Ch1 (master source), Ch2 (`Book/build/chapter-2/chapter.md`, 2,072 words), Ch3 Epoch 2 (`Book/build/law-1/epoch2_chapter.md`, 5,638 words), the Persona Map and 17 cast cards.

## A. The Keeper

1. Ch1 already has the Keeper, unnamed. The pull quote "You do not fail to make lasting change. You succeed at staying who you are." is the Keeper's whole job in one line. So the name may not need a new scene. It may only need to land on a line that already exists.
2. Where is it named first? End of Ch1 (right after the pull quote), Ch2 (with the five Keep negations as a box), or Ch3 (when the payoff of not trying is exposed)? Each gives a different book. Ch1 makes it the book's spine. Ch3 makes it a Law 1 discovery.
3. Metaphor clash. The car runs Ch2 and Ch3, and the left foot on the brake is "something from your past" (Law 2). His definition of the Keeper (hard to move when still, hard to stop when moving) is inertia. That is the weight of the car, not the foot. Is the Keeper the foot, the weight, or the driver who likes the seat? Unresolved.
4. The two sides line up with Ch2's own structure. Laws 1 to 3: the Keeper holds you still. Laws 4 and 5: the same Keeper keeps the new habit rolling (the Shinkansen). "It changes sides" may already be what "Do it, tune it, trust it" is describing. Ch2 could say it in one paragraph.
5. The dark side of "hard to stop" is in his own material: the train still at 100 km/h after the cargo is gone (F-90), doom gaming "to keep my average" (F-43 to F-47), Sarah the sawblade. So the Keeper is not only a starting problem. Does the book show it misbehaving while moving, or only once it is on our side?
6. Sneaky Slimy David is David's private name for his Keeper. That suggests a reader move: everyone's Keeper sounds different. Possible, but From Within You must not repeat a question asked in the chapter.
7. Ch3 section titles almost read as "Keep Their Course" already: Whose Goal Is It, What Do You Get From Not Trying. The negation may name what Ch3 is fighting without new text.
8. Fit with the core belief: the Keeper is a force, not a flaw, and it switches sides. That keeps the reader resourceful and not broken. Worth guarding in every use.

## B. The cast in Ch2 and Ch3

9. Ch2 "Who You'll Meet" promises three people "in every Law". The map has four mains. Bayu is missing from Ch2, and his laws are 5, 1, 3, 4 (no Law 2). So "every Law" is not true for anyone yet. Either introduce four, or change the promise.
10. 17 names is too many for a reader to hold. Clear's pattern: a few recurring people plus many one-time people. Should the one-story cast appear by background first ("a cafe owner in Malaysia") and only get a name if they return?
11. Ch2 carries his thesis (different problems, same place underneath). The Venn is that thesis drawn. One paragraph of quick backgrounds, each saying its own label, could prove it in Ch2 without introducing anyone properly.
12. Ch3 has many "think of" hypotheticals: the evening class, the language app, the two parents, the two students, the crush, the beef jerky, "My son is smart, he's just lazy". The cast can turn some into real people. That is probably the biggest change personas make to Ch3: fewer imagined people, more met ones. The diet pair stays as the frame.
13. Natural homes for the five Law 1 one-stories:
    - Michael in "Whose Goal Is It?" (a goal chosen because it looks cool, parents paid).
    - Putri in "What Do You Get From Not Trying?" ("grades should speak for her" is the exam story made real).
    - Andi in "Choosing Your Direction" (some directions take a year, against David's one page).
    - Setsu in the drawer paragraph (200 pages never posted). But she mirrors Gen's own manuscript, already in the same section. Two of the same in one chapter?
    - Aiman fits Law 1 weakest. His card is Law 1 and 3. Maybe Ch5.
14. Room. Ch3 is 5,638 words. Five one-stories plus three mains plus Joe Liemandt, Fosbury, Schultz is a crowd. Every persona added means a hypothetical or an outside voice removed. Joe goes to once at most already.
15. Map rule: each Law chapter picks 2 or 3 slices. Ch3 as drafted is H (David) with A and G visiting. The one-stories add D and F. Is Ch3 H, D, F, with Melina and Dan as the visitors?
16. Melina is called "another person I coach" in Ch3 after a full introduction in Ch2. Small, but a sign Ch3 was written before Ch2 settled the cast.

## C. The parents thread

17. Every parent in the draft now is a source of borrowed goals or an excuse keeper: the camps, "what my parents wanted", "My son is smart, he's just lazy". Nobody speaks to a parent reader warmly yet, in Intro, Ch2 or Ch3.
18. A real tension. Ch3 says "since I didn't pay, I never gave them my full potential". The seed he wants is that paying for someone's coaching is fine. Michael and Putri were parent-paid and it worked. The line that may resolve it: it was never who paid, it was whose reason. Michael's coaching did not make him do what his parents wanted. It helped him find his own reason, which is what a worried parent wanted all along.
19. Dan already shows "a goal someone hands you can still become yours". With Michael beside him, the same idea covers parents too.
20. Does a parent ever get their own scene (the name pool has Hui Min and Dimas), or only lines addressed to them? A parent's own struggle (the worry, not knowing how to help without pushing) has no home yet.
