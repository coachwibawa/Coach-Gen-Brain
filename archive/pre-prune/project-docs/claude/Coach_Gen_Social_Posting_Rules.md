# Coach Gen Social Posting Rules

Platform-specific adaptation rules for `post-to-socials`. Scope: how a finished piece of content (an article, an Insight, a brainstorm pick) gets reshaped for the mechanics of each platform it's posted to, once it's already in Coach Gen's voice.

**This is a different concern from `claude/Coach_Gen_Content_Rules.md`.** Content Rules governs what the content *is* (voice, shape, the 5x5x5 brainstorm). This doc governs how that finished content gets *formatted for a specific platform's feed mechanics*: link placement, length, hooks, hashtags, sign-offs. Do not merge the two docs. A platform rule here should never reshape the underlying voice; it only adjusts packaging.

**Structure.** One section per platform. Starts with LinkedIn since that's the first one built out. Add a new section per platform as `post-to-socials` grows to support it (Facebook, Instagram, others), same add-and-annotate discipline as the other rulebooks in this project: never condense, keep corrections alongside the original line.

## Platform mechanics (reference, not a style choice, informs the rules above)

- A LinkedIn feed post folds behind "see more" after roughly 140 characters on mobile, 210 on desktop. Everything before that fold is the hook; it decides whether anyone reads the rest.
- Hard post limit is 3,000 characters.
- An external link in the post body measurably reduces reach, studies in 2026 range from about 17% to 60% fewer impressions depending on account type (personal profiles see a smaller hit than company pages). Putting the link in the first comment instead, the old workaround, stopped helping sometime around 2024 and is itself penalized now, few people click a link in comments anyway. If the goal is clicks, the link belongs in the body, full stop.
- Text-only posts in the 700-900 character range tend to perform best; posts can run longer (up to the 3,000 cap) without penalty beyond that sweet spot, but there's no benefit to padding length either.
- Ending on a direct question tends to drive comments, which the algorithm weights heavily.
- (2026-10-15, live run) Typing "#" opens LinkedIn's hashtag suggestions. Typing a space after the last hashtag, before the line break, kept the suggestion from swapping it.

## Platform mechanics (reference)

- Facebook folds a post behind "See more" after about 125 characters on mobile and about 477 on desktop. With an image attached, even less text shows. Hard limit 63,206 characters.
- Posts of about 150-300 words draw the most comments; 50-100 words draw more shares. The first lines decide whether anyone taps "See more".
- For accounts without a big audience, text and image posts give the best return. An image should come with a caption that adds meaning the photo doesn't show.
- The algorithm favors posts that start comment threads. Asking for likes, shares, or follows can hurt reach.
- **Links (researched 2026-10-15).** Meta is testing a cap of 2 link posts per month in the post body for Pages and professional-mode profiles without Meta Verified (a test, not a full rollout); over the cap, links may just get little reach. Links in comments are not capped. One 2026 test on a single Page: link in the first comment got about 9% more reach but about 38% fewer link clicks than the link in the post. The same source says Meta has no fixed rule against links in 2026 and any effect is small and inconsistent. For a Page with almost no followers, a 9% reach difference is close to nothing, so the choice is mainly about clicks.
  - (2026-10-15, first live run) The link cap applies to his Page: the Coach Gen composer shows a "2 links left" chip.
- (2026-10-15, first live run) The Coach Gen composer opens as a full page at `facebook.com/post/create`, with the text box, an "Add photos or videos" area, a live preview on the right, and an "Add AI label" toggle that reads "We require you to label certain realistic content that's made with AI." Whether to switch that toggle on is his call, never Claude's.
- **Hashtags (researched 2026-10-15).** Facebook hashtags don't work as a discovery engine the way Instagram or TikTok tags do. One 8-week test on a 9,000-follower Page: posts with 3 hashtags averaged 12.5% less reach than posts with none, and two drew "spammy" comments. Single-Page evidence. Common advice: 0-3 niche tags at the end, never broad stacks. His current posts use 5 broad tags.
- **Audience (researched 2026-10-15).** Facebook reaches about 203 million users in Indonesia (about 71% of the population, NapoleonCat, Sep 2026). Largest age groups: 25-34 (37.8%), then 18-24 (28.3%), then 35-44 (19.1%). It is the mass-market, mobile-first channel there, not an older-skewed one as in the West.

## Coachinator Script Import parser (reference, from the Antigravity brief, 2026-10-16)

How the parser reads a script. These parsing facts are binding. The brief's content advice (audience, tone, 8-10 slides, @askcoachgen handle, "introvert" on slide 1) is not; the rules above and his voice win.

- Each slide starts with `# Slide N: Type` (or `# Type`). Type keywords: `cover`, `bullets`, `myth` / `lie` / `reality` (Myth-Reality), `quote`, `timeline`, `cta` / `call to action`.
- `key: value` lines, case-insensitive keys: `title` (heading, also timeline title and CTA headline), `subtitle` and `tagline` (Cover), `cta` (button or swipe text), `myth` or `lie`, `reality`, `quote`, `author`, `authortitle` (Quote), `start`, `end` (Timeline), `keyword`, `reward` (CTA). `subheadline:` stays empty: he fills it in the UI.
- Lines starting `- ` or `* `: bullets on Bullets slides, checklist on CTA slides, milestones on Timeline slides as `- [50%]: Label (Star|Diamond|Dot)`.
- Inline marks: `==accent pill==`, `++green reward pill++`, `~~blurred tease~~`, `<br>` line break.
- Line prefixes: `[bold]`, `[accent]`, `[faint]`, `[faint2]`, `[blur]`, `[bullet]`.
- Not in the parser per the brief: Prose, Simple-Quote, Social-Mockup slides (they exist in the editor's Insert New Slide), images, floating pictures, silhouettes, style presets. Those are added by hand in the Slide Editor.
- Claude's own overrides used on the David script (2026-10-16): full English, slide count set by the story (not fixed 8-10), only true on-record claims (no invented lead magnet, keyword, or reward), his locked line instead of an outside quote, handle @officialcoachgen, no em dashes anywhere including `authortitle`.
