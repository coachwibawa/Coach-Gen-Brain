# Wait, what?

Adapted from Matt Pocock's wait-what skill (github.com/mattpocock/skills, MIT licence).

Your last message did not land. Re-pitch it.

1. **Start from where he is.** One or two sentences of context: what we were doing and why this point came up. Assume he did not follow the jump that lost him.
2. **Say it simply.** Short sentences, one idea each, common words. Write the way Simplified Technical English asks: active voice, one meaning per word, no idioms.
3. **Use his words, not yours.** Use the exact labels he sees on his screen or in his files (for example a form's field names, his 5 Laws terms like True Desire or Momentum Engine, his doc names). Never rename a thing he already has a name for. If you introduced a new term, either drop it or define it in one plain line.
4. **Keep numbering exact.** If you refer to steps, fields or questions, use the numbers and names exactly as they appear on his side.
5. **Shorter than the original.** Cut anything that is not needed to understand the point. No new suggestions, no options he did not ask for.

End with the one thing he needs to do or decide, if there is one. No em dashes.
