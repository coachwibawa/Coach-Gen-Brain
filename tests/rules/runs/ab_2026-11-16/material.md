## Entry 406 (WS-TR-Stories-cbb3-L3, own story)

[his own words, typed for the worksheet. The entry also holds a pasted article on another subject after this text. That part is left out.]

At that start of this year, I did pruning to retake back control of my day. because to take control of my life, I need to take control of my day. These were a lot of tasks that are important to me, side hustle with a relative, walking my dog, coaching friends who need it but can't pay for free, maintaining my business' website, entrepreneur community I am part of, side hustle with a different relative, tending to my rose garden, going to the gym that's 45 minutes drive away, walking my dog every morning, side hustle with another different relative, maintaining my Japanese proficiency by talking to natives online, being a great evaluator at toastmasters, my BNI membership, hangout communities I am part of, friend group chats, Inside the Introvert Community. Each of these are projects that I nurtured from 0 to get to where it is today, I didn't start my Japanese learning fluent, so I feel really bad to neglect it now, that it has now bear fruit. But all of this add up, 5 minutes here, 45 minutes there, and there's 20 of these in my life at any given time, I count on my time tracker that there is always 2-3 hours a day spent on these mundane things and just keeping them on top of my working memory. Killing hurts, because of all these things, I can never say that any of these is of no use to my life and well being, nor can I say for sure that it is a fruitless endeavor and the side hustles will not turn into a million dollar business. But I have to do it because it is has crept to the point that every months are filled with the same things with no room for new opportunities. Only after I killed them, I was able to go back with a clear mind one year later, and restart with a fresh seed those that I pruned, but I realized I actually needed in my life.
