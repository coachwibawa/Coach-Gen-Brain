What fail means. This rule is Tier 1: a real break of it fails the whole draft. A real break is a place where a careful reader loses something because of this rule: they are confused, lose the thread, have to guess, stop trusting the writer, or feel the writing is off. Do not fail the draft for polish, for taste, for a line you would only phrase differently, for grammar or spelling slips, or for something a different rule covers. Fail when you can point to the words and say what the reader loses there.

How to judge.
- Read the whole draft first. Judge the draft as a whole, not one line at a time. A rule can break across paragraphs.
- Judge only what is on the page. Some lines of the rule are about how the writer works or about parts this draft may not include (a grey line at the top, an end note, a list made before drafting). If the draft does not include that part, skip those lines and judge the prose.
- Lines in bold with "Insight" are field headings, not prose. Skip them.
- Quote the draft's own words exactly.

How to read this rule, from his verdicts on past drafts:
- You cannot see his records, the brain or the session. Never fail a draft because you cannot tell whether a detail, a feeling or a quote is in the record. Judge what the page does for the reader.
- His own typed wording can be a little rough (an odd word order, a tense slip). If a reader still follows it, that is a grammar matter for another check, not a break of this rule.
- An approved piece he would score 90% can still have lines you would polish. Fail only a break that would make him say the passage fails, not one that would make him edit a line.
- In an article or letter, Insight 1 may end on a question that Insight 2 and 3 go on to answer. Only the end of the whole piece must not be the hard question alone. Insight 3 is a question by design.

A line that turns the story around, so the reader sees their problem was never what they feared ("David's zeros were never laziness"), is that possibility and it promises liberation. Do not fail a piece because the way out is not spelled out as steps.
